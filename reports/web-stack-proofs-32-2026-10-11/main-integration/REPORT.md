# Direct main integration — proof batch32

This work integrates directly into nine `main` branches, without creating PRs. The compiler, knowledge DB, planner, runtime and five lowering packages preserve both the frozen local source checkpoints and the fetched upstream work. The exact source inventory and remote publication receipt accompany this report.

The DB additions prove that two wrapping-integer prefix scans combined elementwise can become one scan over combined inputs. The family covers addition/subtraction, U32/I32/U64, nonzero starts, independently sized arrays, composed helpers and mapped held-out cases. The timed and generated-target recipes are U32; proof admission for I32/U64 does not claim GPU coverage for those types. There are 30 fresh proof objects and 12 reused consequences, giving 42 published roots. Concrete laws remain in the DB. Helper-definition binding, shared-value materialization, destination lowering, representation admission and target execution remain general compiler/package machinery. No generated caller-validation wrapper was introduced.

The canonical DB merge retains 2,459 active objects and 4,206 names. Explicit immutable snapshot queries authenticate their own canonical membership rather than depending on the latest disposable SQLite index. Planner discovery is paginated independently of its checking budget; proposed rewrites and selected dependency closures still pass the ordinary checker. Invalid selected laws and changed helper definitions are rejected.

The integration retains newer upstream owned collections, typed actions, checked physical storage representations and runtime migrations. Production defaults reject historical aggregate/storage admission; archived experiments require the explicit `legacy-optimisation-research` feature. These upstream representation features have their own documented source-domain and backend limits.

The final validation receipt records complete interpreter/proof replay, automatic selection against the full merged catalogue with the 30 fresh objects removed, complete generated JS/Wasm outputs, actual WebGPU execution, owned-output lifetimes and input nonmutation. It also records the full production suite and explicitly flagged historical research tests. Earlier failures and interrupted preparation attempts are preserved alongside their classified fixes and later passing runs.

The [frozen batch32 performance report](../REPORT.md) contains the timing evidence: resident Wasm gains around 1.34× and full WebGPU gains around 1.44× against prior Ink on this workload family, with regressions retained. Strong handwritten JS/Rust controls still win most comparisons; the results do not establish general native-performance superiority. The merged-main replay is a correctness/application check with no timed benchmark samples. Frozen measurements are not attributed to the newer compiler.

Original proof, source and benchmark bytes remain in their archived checkpoints.
